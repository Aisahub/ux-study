She left school at sixteen, has never worked in an office, and has never been asked to help test anything. She is reading this on her phone, behind the counter, between customers, after tapping a link in an email from Marigold.

We are building the next version of Marigold, and before it reaches anyone's shop we want to watch a few shopkeepers use it. That is what this page is asking you for: about forty minutes, once, on a video call. We call that a session.

What a session is like

You will be using Marigold the way you always do — there is nothing new to learn and nothing to install.

The task is short: open the Stock tab, tap Stock check, and work down the list until every count matches.

Each session runs over a video call with one person from our team, and takes about forty minutes. The call is recorded and kept in our UT archive for six months; only the product team can open it.

There is nothing to prepare and nothing to get right. If something confuses you, that is the most useful thing that can happen in the whole forty minutes.

Who we are looking for

We are looking for shopkeepers who do stock reconciliation — counting what is actually on the shelves against what Marigold says is there — at least once a month.

You do not need to be quick with a phone, and you do not need to have used every part of Marigold. Shops run by one or two people are exactly who we have the fewest of.

How it works

  1. You tell us a little about your shop.
  2. We look for a session that suits the hours you keep.
  3. A coordinator settles the time with you.
  4. You join the call from your phone.

Once you are in, a coordinator sends you a Slotwise so you can pick a time.

Everyone who takes part gets a $40 voucher for a shop of their choosing. We send it the day after the call.

You can stop at any point in the call, and you can change your mind before it without telling us why. Nothing about your Marigold account changes either way.

What came out of the last round

“The stock screen feels cluttered and a bit dated — it could really do with a cleanup.”

A shopkeeper in the spring round. We agreed, and we gave it a refresh.

Two other things people ran into in the spring — the way a part-delivered order was counted, and the wording on the low-stock alert — are both changed in the version you would be looking at.
